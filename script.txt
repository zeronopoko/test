The front door clicks shut behind us. In the entrance hall, Ichigo stops walking and I finally let go of his leg. My head is still swimming. I shuffle out of Yuzu’s sandals, line them up next to his shoes, and wander into the living room.

I flop onto the sofa face-first, then roll onto my back and stare at the ceiling. The room tilts a little. I let it.

Behind me, Ichigo lets out a grunt. Isshin steps in, plucks Yuzu off his back as if she weighs nothing, and cradles her against his chest. The two of them head upstairs with Karin, helping the girls settle into bed.

Floorboards creak overhead, then a door, then the rustle of blankets. I stay on the sofa and listen to the house settle.

A few minutes later they come back down. Isshin stops at the foot of the stairs and rubs the back of his neck. “Now, where do we put Mochi-chan?” He glances at me, then at the sofa. “Well, she could sleep there, but...” He frowns and shakes his head. “I’d hate leaving a small girl on a sofa all night. That’s no way to treat a guest.” His face brightens. “I know! Ichigo, you take the sofa, and she can have your bed!”

“No.” Ichigo doesn’t even pause to think about it. “I’ve had ten nights of terrible sleep, and I still ache everywhere. I’m not sleeping on a couch.”

Isshin blinks at him. “You’re really going to make a little girl sleep on the sofa while you keep your bed?”

Ichigo exhales through his nose. “I’ve got the closet. It’s big enough, and there’s already bedding in there. She can use that.”

Isshin gapes at him. His jaw hangs open and one hand rises to his chest, as if he’s been shot. “You’d put a girl in a closet? Not only that but your own sister too?!” He wags a trembling finger. “You’re a terrible brother!”

Ichigo doesn’t bother denying it. He seems to realize that Isshin has already started treating me as family, and that arguing the point would take all night. He looks at me instead.

I turn my head on the sofa cushion and look at the two of them. Then I answer in a flat, plain voice.

“Mm. Closet is fine.”

Isshin deflates like a punctured balloon. His arm drops, and his shoulders sag. For a second he looks as though he’d like to argue, but he only sighs and nods slowly. “...Well. If that’s what she wants.” He waves a hand toward the stairs. “Go on, then. Don’t make her walk in the dark.”

Ichigo crosses the room and stops by the sofa. He doesn’t offer a hand, and he doesn’t say anything. He just stands there until I swing my legs down and get up. The floor wobbles. I steady myself against the sofa arm, then follow him to the stairs.

The upstairs hall is dark, with a faint line of light under one door. Ichigo opens the last one on the left and holds it for me.
